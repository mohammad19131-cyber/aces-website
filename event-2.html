Ad Making

Advertisement Creation & Ad Defense

Ad Making tests delegates’ creativity, marketing insight, storytelling ability, and their capacity to defend advertising decisions under pressure.

Competition Rounds

Round 1 — Advertisement Creation

Round 1 focuses on testing delegates’ creativity, marketing insight, and storytelling ability. Teams are required to conceptualize, film, and edit a short advertisement for an allotted product within a limited time frame. The objective is to assess how effectively teams can communicate a product’s value, appeal to a target audience, and deliver a compelling message under time constraints.

Each team will be assigned a product at the start of the round and must plan, film, and edit a 60–90 second advertisement entirely on campus and within the given time limit. The advertisement may follow any creative approach—story-based, informational, emotional, or comedic—as long as it clearly promotes the product and aligns with basic advertising principles.

During production, teams may script, shoot, and edit their advertisement using permitted devices and software, but no pre-recorded clips, external assistance, or external assets may be used. At the end of the round, teams must submit their final edited advertisement within the allotted time.

Teams will be evaluated on their understanding of the product, clarity of the central message, effectiveness in storytelling, originality and creativity of the concept, appeal to the intended target audience, and technical execution, including video quality, audio clarity, and editing.

Judging Criteria
Product understandingMessage and storytellingCreativity and originalityTechnical appeal and execution
Round 2 — Ad Defense

Number of qualified teams: 20

In this round, qualifying teams are assigned a controversial advertisement that has faced criticism because of its messaging, tone, ethics, cultural sensitivity, or public reception. Teams are given a fixed preparation time to analyze the advertisement, identify the core controversies surrounding it, and construct a structured defense from the perspective of the brand or advertising agency responsible.

During the defense, teams must justify the intent, creative choices, and strategic value of the advertisement while addressing ethical and public criticism with logical and persuasive arguments. Pitches should demonstrate a clear understanding of the advertisement’s concept, target audience, and alignment with brand identity or marketing goals.

Judges may challenge teams through follow-up questions to test their ability to think critically, respond under pressure, and defend their position confidently. Scores from this round are combined with Round 1 to determine the final rankings, including the winner, runners-up, and third place.

Judging Criteria
Strategic justificationEthical and social reasoningPersuasion and communicationTeam coordination and clarity
