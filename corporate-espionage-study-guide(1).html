Investigation & Corporate Intelligence

Corporate Espionage

Case File Analysis & Crime Board Investigation

Corporate Espionage challenges teams to investigate a corporate incident, distinguish useful evidence from deliberate misdirection, identify suspects and motives, and build a coherent theory from fragmented information.

Competition Rounds

Round 1 — Case File Analysis

In this round, each team receives a confidential case file containing company background, incident summaries, employee profiles, internal communications, security logs, and media leaks. Not all information is relevant, as some are included to mislead delegates. Teams have one hour to submit a formal investigative report covering a high-level overview and initial assessment, the nature of the incident, such as a data breach, sabotage, or whistleblower death, the overall risk posed to the corporation, central suspicions, and critical red flags.

Delegates must analyze emails, financial records, security logs, and other documents to identify inconsistencies and unusual patterns, cross-referencing evidence where possible. Teams are also required to create a chronological timeline of events, showing escalation from corporate tension to criminal activity, and shortlist two to three prime suspects, analyzing their role, motive, means, opportunity, and counterpoints.

Finally, teams must present a preliminary theory explaining what they believe happened, whether the act appears individual or coordinated, and how corporate espionage fits into the incident.

Round 2 — Cipher Room Investigation & Crime Board

Number of qualified teams: 20

Teams progress through three phases: the cipher and evidence room, witness interrogation, and crime board construction. In the first phase, teams enter the investigation room in groups of five for a total of six minutes, solving ciphers such as substitution codes, logic puzzles, and corporate-themed encryptions. The room contains locked files, sealed envelopes, physical clues, and red herrings. Each solved cipher unlocks critical evidence or access to a witness for interrogation.

In the second phase, teams conduct interrogations with witnesses, who provide confirmations, partial truths, or misleading information depending on how questions are asked. Teams must manage time per witness and question sequencing effectively.

In the third phase, teams use only the evidence obtained to construct a crime board that visually and logically connects suspects, motives, evidence trails, timelines, and methods of responsibility, indicating the final accused parties and supporting evidence. Teams then present their crime board in two to three minutes and may be questioned by the judging panel. Delegates are provided with crime boards, chart papers, printed photos, pins, strings, markers, and other materials, while the room contains props such as mannequins, fake blood, UV torches, and other investigative tools.
