Business Strategy & Negotiation

Rubicon

Strategic Alliances & Partnership Reckoning

Rubicon tests teams’ ability to negotiate, collaborate, make strategic compromises, and defend business decisions under pressure.

Competition Rounds

Round 1 — Strategic Alliance

In this round, participants are tested on their ability to negotiate, collaborate, and reach mutually beneficial agreements under structured constraints. Teams are placed in a realistic business environment where cooperation is essential to unlock value that cannot be achieved independently.

Each round functions as a two-team negotiation exercise in which paired teams represent different companies facing a shared business opportunity with aligned goals but conflicting interests. Teams are given a detailed case study outlining company objectives, strengths, and limitations, requiring them to identify shared objectives, recognize conflicts of interest, and negotiate partnership terms such as profit sharing, roles, control, and resource allocation.

After an initial preparation phase, teams engage in live negotiations with their paired counterpart, making strategic compromises while defending their company’s priorities. By the end of the negotiation period, teams must finalize and jointly present a clear partnership agreement, which may take the form of a joint venture, strategic partnership, or contractual collaboration. Advancement is determined by the clarity, feasibility, and mutual benefit of the agreement, evaluated through factors such as profit potential, exposure, and marketing value.

Judging Criteria
Communication and persuasionStrategic compromiseAlignment of incentivesProblem-solving through collaboration
Round 2 — The Weakest Link: Partnership Reckoning

Number of qualified teams: 18

In the final round, all teams that qualified from Round 1 compete individually in a fast-paced, elimination-based challenge inspired by The Weakest Link. Although partnerships were formed in the previous round, teams now compete independently and must rely solely on their own understanding, decisions, and performance.

All teams are seated together while judges conduct rapid question cycles focused on the partnerships formed in Round 1, including their terms, trade-offs, risks, and strategic implications. Questions may be direct, scenario-based, or comparative, requiring teams to defend or critique partnership decisions under pressure.

At the end of each question cycle, judges deliberate and publicly eliminate one team as “the weakest link.” This process continues until only three teams remain. In the final stage, judges continue questioning all remaining teams without further eliminations, assessing accuracy, strategic reasoning, confidence, adaptability, and consistency across responses. Final rankings are determined based on overall performance, with the strongest team declared the winner, followed by second and third place.

Judging Criteria
Accuracy and understandingStrategic reasoning and business judgmentClarity and communicationAdaptability and consistency
